## Recommended manuscript paragraph

To quantify the incremental contribution of graph-derived information to symptom prioritization, we performed a component ablation at the symptom-topic level. The no-graph event-association score was the standardized Elastic Net permutation importance, whereas the graph-augmented score combined standardized Elastic Net permutation importance and the GNN event-loss delta with equal weights. The GNN event-loss delta was defined as the change in binary cross-entropy event loss after removing all message–symptom edges incident to each symptom topic; positive values indicated worse model fit after removal. For structural importance, the no-graph score averaged standardized symptom coverage, recency-weighted coverage, and mean message–symptom edge weight, while the graph-augmented score additionally included symptom-node PageRank. We compared graph-augmented and no-graph rankings using Spearman and Kendall correlations, mean absolute rank change, top-10 and top-20 overlap, and overlap of high-risk candidate sets. High-risk candidates required a positive Elastic Net coefficient and event-association and structural-importance scores at or above the 80th percentile. This ablation assessed the incremental contribution of graph-derived components to symptom ranking; it was not interpreted as evidence that the GNN improved held-out patient-level discrimination.

## Short response-to-reviewer version

We added a graph-component ablation that recomputed the symptom rankings after removing the GNN event-loss delta from the event-association score and PageRank from the structural-importance score. We then compared the graph-augmented and no-graph rankings using rank correlations, absolute rank shifts, top-k overlap, and overlap of the defined high-risk candidate sets. This analysis isolates how graph-derived information changes symptom prioritization, while the separately reported out-of-fold AUROC and AUPRC evaluate patient-level discrimination.
